Lunch picker — what to eat when nobody can decide

Picking lunch takes forever not because there are too many options, but because nobody wants to be the one who chose.

한국어 English 日本語

Everyone says "I don't mind", then every suggestion gets "we had that yesterday". Choosing lunch drags because nobody wants to be the person who picked. Handing the decision to chance is faster.

Shortlist first, then draw

The common mistake with a random picker is to throw in every option. Then something one person cannot eat comes up, and you spin again anyway.

  • Veto first. Each person removes one option they really do not want — something they cannot eat, or what they had yesterday.
  • Put in only what is left. Three to eight options works well.
  • Go with what comes up. Everything left on the list is something everyone already accepted.

Racing the menu

Race Roulette works with dishes or places instead of people. Enter "Ramen", "Tacos", "Salad bar" — up to ten characters each — and the options race; whatever finishes first is lunch.

The advantage over a wheel is that you do not know until the end. Places swap in the last few seconds, so watching it becomes cheering rather than deciding. You also see second and third place, so if the winner turns out to be closed, go with the runner-up.

Giving the popular choice more weight

If most people lean one way, use weights — one booster per person who wants it. Three people want ramen? Give ramen 3 boosters. Its chance becomes 3 ÷ the total of all boosters. It is more likely, not guaranteed, so the minority choice still has a shot.

If you do this every day

  • The list is cleared when you reload. Keep your usual options in a note and paste them in.
  • Drop yesterday's winner from today's list to avoid repeats.
  • Want to settle who pays as well? See a fair game to decide who pays.
