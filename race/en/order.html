Random order generator — decide who goes first, fairly

Deciding an order is not picking one name five times. It is drawing the whole line at once.

한국어 English 日本語

Presentation order, whose turn it is in a board game, the cleaning rota. Ask "who wants to go first?" and nobody moves. So you pick at random — and depending on how you do it, there is grumbling afterwards or there is not.

What goes wrong with one name at a time

The usual way is to spin a wheel for first place, remove that name, spin again for second, and so on. The odds are fine. But six people means five spins, and five chances for "that spin didn't count, my finger slipped".

Setting an order is really drawing the whole line in one go. One draw leaves no room for re-spins, and everyone watches the same thing once.

Using a race to set the order

Race Roulette draws the full ranking of every participant at once, then plays a race in which the runners cross the line in exactly that order. First to last is your order.

  • Enter the names — 2 to 12 of them, up to ten characters each.
  • Press Start and watch together.
  • The results board is the order. First place presents first, second presents second — or whatever you agreed.

With a lot of people a race takes longer; use 2× or 4× speed, or set laps to 1. Nothing is saved, so screenshot the results board if you need to keep it.

Two things to agree before you start

  • Does first place go first or last? Going first is often the worse slot, so some groups let the winner choose last. Either way, say it before the race. Deciding after seeing the result makes the draw unfair.
  • No re-runs. "Best of three" only helps whoever lost the first one.

Taking last time into account

If someone went first last week and you want them less likely to go first again, give them fewer boosters (a lower weight) than everyone else. The lower the weight, the lower the chance of finishing near the front. Boosters are shown on screen, so everyone can see who got what.

A weight of 0 means they effectively never win. Unless you want them out, use small differences like 1 and 2 instead.

More than twelve people

A race holds up to twelve, so for a whole class you split into groups first. How to split fairly and how to draw several times without repeats is in when you need more than one pick.
